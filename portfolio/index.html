VA Service Ticket Tracker
Miller Information Systems · 2023–2026 · Google Forms, Sheets, OCR, Zapier, Trello

Problem: Two VA hospitals sent paper service tickets, about 120 a month, with no searchable record. Techs were sometimes dispatched to the same issue twice.

Built: Techs photographed each ticket. OCR turned the image into text, spreadsheet formulas pulled out the fields, duplicates were dropped by ticket number, and each ticket landed on a Trello board.

Result: Duplicate dispatches stopped, and the workload numbers were used in contract renegotiation. This was my first AI-built tool, put together entirely through chat with ChatGPT before AI coding tools existed.

Hirsch to Genetec Cardholder Migration
Miller Information Systems · SQL export, Excel, UiPath, Genetec Security Center

Problem: A client moving access control systems had 1,332 cardholders. Reissuing badges or retyping records by hand wasn't an option.

Built: Pulled the credential data out of the old system's SQL database, cleaned and mapped it in Excel, and loaded it into Genetec with a UiPath automation, testing on 20 records, then 100, then all.

Result: Zero errors. New badges not required.

Quote-to-Purchase-Order Splitter
Miller Information Systems · Excel VBA, written with ChatGPT

Problem: Large quotes had to be broken into purchase orders under a client's $2,500 card limit, by hand, every week.

Built: An Excel macro that splits a quote into as few purchase orders as possible under the limit.

Result: Weekly quoting time went from about 4 hours to 30 minutes.

Telecom Cost Recovery Program
Education Management Corporation · 2011–2016 · Excel, carrier portals, call reports, SharePoint

Problem: As campuses opened and moved, bills kept arriving for phone lines and circuits nobody could explain. Services had been ordered for years with no central record.

Built: A process for tracing every billed line, across PDFs, portals, and CSVs in a different format from every vendor, to the number or circuit behind it and where it actually rang. Doubtful lines moved to a central SIP circuit instead of being disconnected, so nothing in use was cut off. A per-campus inventory on SharePoint kept every service documented.

Result: More than $2.5M in yearly savings, and I was named the company's sole authorized ordering agent with its telecom and data carriers.

FrameSeam (video cutter)
2026 · Electron, ffmpeg, Claude Code · GitHub

Problem: I cut long recordings down regularly. Clipchamp's layout was right for the job, but its exports top out at 30 fps, it kept pushing projects into OneDrive, and its cache had quietly taken 35 GB of my drive.

Built: A Clipchamp-style cutter built around one rule: the export is the original video, 1:1, with only the deleted parts removed. Resolution, frame rate (60 and 120 fps included), orientation and HDR color carry through untouched. Cuts land on the exact frame, clips can be joined, and encoding runs on the GPU (NVIDIA, Intel or AMD, detected automatically) with a picker for PCs that have more than one card. A lossless mode skips re-encoding entirely. Everything runs locally.

Result: The idea, the interface brief and the testing are mine; Claude wrote the code to that brief. It's open source on GitHub.

Pittsburgh Food Specials (live proof of concept)
2026 · Claude agents, Airtable, Gmail drafts, Cloudflare · live site

Daily PC Health System
2026 · PowerShell, scheduled tasks, Claude

My main PC kept shutting off while I was away. The logs pointed to the power button, almost certainly pressed by my cat. I disabled the button, and the fix grew into a daily health system. A monitor logs temperatures, fans and load every 30 seconds and runs a custom fan curve on the main graphics card. Every morning an agent reviews crashes, hardware errors, disk health, updates and remote access, fixes what it can from a fixed list of safe actions, and shows me a green, yellow or red summary on login. Anything that needs a decision stays with me, with a recommendation attached.
